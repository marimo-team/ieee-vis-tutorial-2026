# References

These are the sources we found while starting the tutorial plan on November 9, 2026.

## IEEE VIS

The [tutorial description](https://www.ieeevis.org/year/2026/info/program/tutorials/) is aimed at authors of interactive visualization systems. It describes building widgets with anywidget, using them with marimo, and sharing them across notebook environments. The [program](https://portal.ieeevis.org/year/2026/program/sessions/?type=tutorial) lists two Wednesday sessions, which we are treating as two halves of the tutorial.

## Previous tutorials

- [SciPy 2026: Interactive computing with marimo and anywidget](https://github.com/manzt/scipy-2026-anywidget) is the main inspiration for the introduction. `nb00_tour.py` introduces marimo; `nb02_interact.py` covers interactivity and widgets; and `nb03_build.py` builds a widget through a series of exercises. The original tutorial was four hours, so we'll need to select the parts that fit. The [conference listing](https://pretalx.com/scipy-2026/speaker/7D3LYU/) describes its scope.
- [SciPy 2024: Bring your __repr__’s to life with anywidget](https://github.com/manzt/scipy-2024-anywidget) uses Jupyter and introduces the connection between Python and the browser. It may be useful for explaining JavaScript and building up the counter example. The [abstract](https://cfp.scipy.org/2024/talk/PLWHZF/) includes its topics and prerequisites.
- [marimo lunch and learn](https://github.com/manzt/marimo-lunch-and-learn) contains introductory and exploration notebooks referenced in the Broad talk.

These are the two tutorial repositories we confirmed in the search. We did not find another anywidget tutorial repository.

## Talks and examples

- [Notebooks as a Tool, Broad Institute](https://github.com/manzt/marimo-broad-talk) is a private repository with slides and demo notebooks. The talk moves from notebooks for people to notebooks for agents. Its discussion of a live notebook and the file it produces could help introduce Kiran's agent demo.
- The private [SciPy 2026 talk repository](https://github.com/manzt/scipy-2026-talk) has a working outline that presents anywidget as a way to connect Python and the web. Its demo moves from reactivity to building a widget, and its discussion of running widgets in different environments could inform the opening and closing.
- The [CHI 2024 human-notebook interactions demo](https://github.com/manzt/anywidget-chi-hni-demo) is another public anywidget example to review.
- The [anywidget repository](https://github.com/manzt/anywidget) includes a minimal counter and links to the project papers. [Built with anywidget](https://github.com/manzt/built-with-anywidget) may provide examples for the opening slides.
- [UpSet.js](https://github.com/upsetjs/upsetjs) is the visualization library proposed for Kiran's examples. We haven't yet worked out the integration.

The Broad and SciPy talk repositories are private. Their links are here for reference; check before copying their material into this repository.
